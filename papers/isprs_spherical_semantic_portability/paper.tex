\title{Frozen Perspective Classifiers as Spherical Semantic Direction Sensors:
A Training-Free Evaluation on Equirectangular Panoramas}
\date{}
\author{***** (anonymous review manuscript)}
\address{**** (affiliation withheld for double-blind review)}

\abstract{
Perspective-pretrained image classifiers contain useful semantic evidence, but
applying them to a $360^\circ$ panorama normally requires cubemaps, overlapping
perspective crops, or retraining.  This paper studies a narrower question: can
their frozen weights be converted into native spherical, fully convolutional
direction sensors without training?  We replace every spatial convolution and
pooling operation by a differentiable tangent-sampling counterpart on an
equirectangular lattice, retain all learned parameters, and convert each global
classifier into a dense score head.  Three complementary semantic interfaces
are evaluated: 1,000 ImageNet object classes, 365 Places365 scene classes, and
caller-defined OpenCLIP RN50 text prompts.  On two native $512\!\times\!1024$
CC0 panoramas, the methods produce $16\!\times\!32$ score lattices directly on
the sphere.  The fixed-label ResNet18 conversions reproduce their unchanged
perspective classifiers at the canonical input size with maximum absolute
errors below $10^{-6}$; the OpenCLIP pointwise value/output projection is exact,
although its dense head is not equivalent to the original global attention
pool.  Mean one-thread CPU inference times are 0.639, 0.678, and 0.996 seconds,
respectively.  The qualitative examples show plausible semantic directions,
but are deliberately selected and do not establish localisation accuracy.  No
vision-language or generative oracle is used.  The contribution is therefore a
reproducible feasibility result and an evaluation protocol, not a claim of
supervised panoramic segmentation performance.
}

\keywords{Spherical convolution, Equirectangular panorama, Class activation map, ImageNet, Places365, OpenCLIP.}

\section{Introduction}

Omnidirectional cameras record a complete viewing sphere in one image.  Their
equirectangular representation is convenient for storage, but its horizontal
and vertical coordinates are longitude and latitude rather than a uniformly
sampled Euclidean plane.  A conventional convolution therefore changes its
angular footprint with latitude and introduces an artificial boundary at the
longitude seam.  A common engineering response is to render several
perspective views, run an ordinary network on every view, and merge the
outputs.  That route is effective, but creates a view-selection problem,
duplicates content in overlapping crops, and makes both memory use and latency
depend on the number of faces.

This work investigates a training-free alternative.  A perspective classifier
is first made fully convolutional in the sense of Long et al.
\cite{long2015fcn}.  Its spatial operators are then evaluated in tangent frames
on the sphere while the learned kernels, biases, normalisation parameters and
residual topology remain frozen.  The resulting score at a lattice position is
interpreted as evidence for the viewing direction represented by that pixel.
For architectures ending in global average pooling and a linear classifier,
the dense pre-pooling scores are the class activation maps (CAMs) of Zhou et
al. \cite{zhou2016cam}.

The study compares three semantic regimes rather than three minor architectural
variants.  ImageNet ResNet18 supplies a fixed object vocabulary
\cite{deng2009imagenet,he2016resnet}; Places365 ResNet18 supplies a fixed scene
vocabulary \cite{zhou2018places}; and OpenCLIP RN50 supplies an open text
interface derived from CLIP \cite{radford2021clip}.  Together they test whether
the same spherical adapter can support object evidence, place evidence, and
prompt-conditioned evidence.

The contributions are: (1) a parameter-preserving conversion from frozen
perspective spatial layers to differentiable spherical operators; (2) a clear
separation between exact dense conversion for average-pool classifiers and the
approximate local interpretation required by CLIP attention pooling; (3) a
hash-pinned pilot comparison of geometry, numerical equivalence, runtime,
memory, and semantic interfaces; and (4) an evaluation boundary that rejects
untraceable model-based judgement.  The paper intentionally makes no claim
that selected visualisations measure segmentation or localisation accuracy.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Dense localisation from classifiers}

Fully convolutional networks replace fixed-size dense layers by convolutional
operators, allowing a network trained with fixed crops to process larger images
and emit a correspondingly larger score lattice \cite{long2015fcn}.  CAM
connects a class score to the last spatial feature tensor when global average
pooling is followed by a linear layer \cite{zhou2016cam}.  These ideas require
no bounding-box or pixel annotation, but their maps are generally coarse: map
resolution is determined by the cumulative output stride, not by the training
crop size.  A $512\!\times\!1024$ input and output stride 32 consequently yield
$16\!\times\!32$ native evidence cells.  Bilinear enlargement improves display
readability but cannot recover evidence removed by downsampling.

\subsection{Learning and inference on the sphere}

Spherical CNN research has addressed projection distortion through learned
sampling, latitude-aware filters, and alternative image domains.  Su and
Grauman transfer perspective features to $360^\circ$ imagery with spherical
convolution \cite{su2017spherical}; SphereNet adapts kernel sampling locations
to equirectangular geometry \cite{coors2018spherenet}; and distortion-aware
filters learn offsets for panoramic dense prediction
\cite{tateno2018distortion}.  Tangent images instead cover the sphere using
locally perspective charts \cite{eder2020tangent}.  These methods motivate the
local tangent construction used here, but the present objective is different:
we do not learn a panoramic model or create a bank of tangent images.  We
evaluate frozen two-dimensional kernels directly on one equirectangular tensor.

\subsection{Complementary semantic vocabularies}

ImageNet classification is object-centred, whereas Places365 was constructed
for scene recognition \cite{deng2009imagenet,zhou2018places}.  CLIP aligns image
and language representations and therefore allows the evaluated vocabulary to
be supplied at inference time \cite{radford2021clip}.  These regimes answer
different questions.  A low score for an indoor scene label in ImageNet is not
evidence that spherical convolution failed; the label may simply not exist in
that classifier.  Conversely, open prompts remove the fixed-vocabulary
constraint but introduce prompt-set dependence and, for dense CLIP, an
architecture-specific approximation.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Equirectangular geometry}

Let an output lattice site represent longitude $\lambda\in[-\pi,\pi)$ and
latitude $\phi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]$.  With $+X$ right, $+Y$ up, and $+Z$ forward,
its unit ray is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{r}(\lambda,\phi)=
 (\sin\lambda\cos\phi,\;\sin\phi,\;\cos\lambda\cos\phi)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The local east and north basis vectors are
\begin{align}
 \mathbf{e}&=(\cos\lambda,0,-\sin\lambda)^{\mathsf T},\\
 \mathbf{n}&=(-\sin\lambda\sin\phi,\cos\phi,
              -\cos\lambda\sin\phi)^{\mathsf T}.
\end{align}
For a kernel offset $(u,v)$, scaled by the angular sampling step and dilation,
define $\mathbf{t}=u\mathbf{e}+v\mathbf{n}$ and $\rho=\|\mathbf{t}\|_2$.  The
sample ray follows the exponential map,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{q}=\cos\rho\,\mathbf{r}+
             \frac{\sin\rho}{\rho}\,\mathbf{t},
 \label{eq:expmap}
\end{equation}
using the continuous limit at $\rho=0$.  The ray is converted back to
longitude and latitude and bilinearly sampled from the equirectangular tensor.
Longitude is circular; latitude stays on the sphere and the limiting pole row
uses border sampling.  All operations, including grid construction and
sampling, remain differentiable.

For input feature tensor $x$, kernel $W$, and bias $b$, the spherical response
at direction $p$ is
\begin{equation}
 y_o(p)=b_o+\sum_i\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}}
          W_{oik}\,x_i\!\left(\Pi(\mathbf{q}_{p,k})\right),
 \label{eq:sconv}
\end{equation}
where $\Pi$ denotes equirectangular projection.  Equation
\ref{eq:sconv} changes only where the frozen kernel samples; the learned
weights are not interpolated, re-estimated, or fine-tuned.  Max and average
pooling use the same tangent neighbourhood construction.  Normalisation,
activations, additions, and pointwise operations are unchanged.

\subsection{Dense heads for the three semantic regimes}

\textbf{ImageNet and Places365.}  Both ResNet18 models end with global average
pooling and a linear classifier.  The linear weights of shape $C\times D$ are
viewed as a $C\times D\times1\times1$ convolution.  Applying this head before
pooling yields one class-score channel per label.  A panorama-level logit is
computed by solid-angle-weighted averaging,
\begin{equation}
 \bar{s}_c=\frac{\sum_{h,w}s_c(h,w)\cos\phi_h}
                  {W\sum_h\cos\phi_h},
 \label{eq:area}
\end{equation}
so oversampled polar rows do not dominate the score.  At the canonical
perspective input, the conversion is required to reproduce the unchanged
classifier before any spherical spatial replacement.

\textbf{OpenCLIP.}  The RN50 visual tower ends with attention pooling over a
fixed $7\!\times\!7$ feature grid.  A dense, variable-resolution interpretation
cannot preserve that complete global operation because its query/key attention
and absolute positional embedding couple all tokens at the training lattice.
We retain the exact learned value projection and output projection, apply them
at every final feature site, $L_2$-normalise the local embeddings, and take
scaled cosine similarity with normalised text embeddings.  This produces a
prompt-score lattice, but it is explicitly \emph{not} the original global CLIP
classification score.  The query/key projections and positional embedding are
not used by the dense head.

\subsection{Visualisation}

For a requested fixed class or text prompt, positive evidence is retained,
min--max normalised per panorama, bilinearly enlarged only for display, and
overlaid on the original image.  The quantitative geometry remains the native
$16\!\times\!32$ lattice.  This avoids suggesting that interpolation creates
fine localisation.  Model ranks are computed from the spherical averages in
Equation~\ref{eq:area}, not from the colourised overlay.

\section{Experimental Protocol}

\subsection{Models, data, and implementation}

The frozen checkpoints are ImageNet ResNet18, the official Places365 ResNet18,
and the OpenAI RN50 weights distributed for OpenCLIP.  Checkpoint identity was
verified by SHA-256 before loading.  The adapters replace every conventional
spatial convolution, max pool, and average pool; an audit rejects a conversion
that leaves any planar spatial layer.  Batch normalisation and all parameters
remain frozen.

The integration pilot uses two $512\!\times\!1024$ CC0 equirectangular images:
an outdoor nature-reserve/forest scene and an indoor studio scene.  They were
chosen to expose different semantic behaviour, not sampled as a statistical
benchmark.  All six maps in Figure~\ref{fig:gallery} were deliberately selected
after inspection to communicate what each semantic interface can express.
Consequently, their labels and apparent localisation are qualitative examples,
not estimates of expected performance.

Experiments used Python 3.12.4, PyTorch 2.4.0, Torchvision 0.19.0, OpenCLIP
2.32.0, an Apple arm64 CPU, and one inference thread.  Timing covers one native
spherical forward pass.  Peak resident memory is process-level and therefore
includes the Python runtime, libraries, model, and temporary tensors.  No
perspective faces are generated or retained.  Parameter counts refer to each
dense inference module; for OpenCLIP this excludes the text encoder after its
prompt embeddings have been computed.

\subsection{Evaluation hierarchy}

Four evidence levels are kept separate.

\begin{enumerate}
\setlength\itemsep{0pt}
\setlength\parskip{0pt}
\item \emph{Structural portability}: number of replaced spatial layers and
      number of conventional spatial layers remaining.
\item \emph{Canonical numerical parity}: maximum absolute error between the
      dense conversion aggregated at canonical input size and the unchanged
      perspective classifier.  For OpenCLIP, parity covers only the retained
      tokenwise value/output projection.
\item \emph{Native execution}: finite outputs, map geometry, wall time, and peak
      resident memory on the two full panoramas.
\item \emph{Semantic evidence}: rank of a selected label or prompt, with maps
      displayed as explicitly selected qualitative cases.
\end{enumerate}

No large language model, vision-language judge, generative model, or human
pseudo-label is used as an oracle.  In particular, OpenCLIP is one of the
methods under test and is never used to score another method.

\subsection{Mask metrics without an oracle}

When paired official masks become available, a class score map $s_c$ will first
be rectified to $a_c=\max(0,s_c)$ without per-image min--max scaling.  Let
$m_c(h,w)\in\{0,1\}$ denote the matching semantic mask and
$\omega_h=\cos\phi_h$ the solid-angle factor.  The fraction of positive
evidence falling inside the class is then
\begin{equation}
 M_c=\frac{\sum_{h,w}\omega_h a_c(h,w)m_c(h,w)}
            {\sum_{h,w}\omega_h a_c(h,w)+\epsilon}.
 \label{eq:mass}
\end{equation}
This metric answers whether the model assigns its evidence to the correct
directions while remaining independent of a chosen threshold.  It must be
reported together with a spherical pointing-game indicator, which is one only
when the highest-scoring direction lies in $m_c$, and with pixelwise average
precision and ROC AUC using $\omega_h$ as sample weight.  For IoU, thresholds
will be swept over the unnormalised score distribution and both the full curve
and its best value reported; the best threshold is descriptive and must not be
reused as an unbiased test setting.  Bootstrap intervals will resample
panoramas, not pixels, because pixels within one scene are strongly dependent.
These definitions make the semantic assessment reproducible from dataset
labels alone and preserve the different area represented by ERP rows.

\subsection{Registered labelled-data evaluation}

A second protocol was prepared for Stanford 2D--3D--S, whose indoor spaces and
semantic annotations derive from the large-scale parsing work of Armeni et al.
\cite{armeni2016stanford}.  Nine ImageNet labels were mapped in advance to five
coarse target classes: bookcase, chair, door, sofa, and table.  Two native
$2048\!\times\!4096$ panoramas and three ImageNet backbones produced 54 target
CAMs.  However, the licensed local dataset copy did not contain matching
official \texttt{pano\_semantic} files.  The evaluator therefore returned
\texttt{ready=false}; intersection-over-union, precision--recall, area under
the ROC curve, and saliency-mass metrics were not computed.  Substituting a
model-generated judgement would violate the protocol.  This registered but
unreported branch documents exactly what data are still required for a future
semantic-accuracy experiment.

\subsection{Native panorama execution}

All methods process both $512\!\times\!1024$ inputs directly and emit finite
$16\!\times\!32$ maps (Table~\ref{tab:runtime}).  Thus map size grows with
input size as expected for a fully convolutional network, while retaining the
backbone output stride of 32.  OpenCLIP is slower and uses more resident memory
than either ResNet18, consistent with its larger parameter count and greater
number of spherical spatial operations.  The difference between ImageNet and
Places365 is small because both share the same ResNet18 topology.

\section{Discussion}

\subsection{What is established}

The experiment establishes three implementation facts.  First, conventional
global-average-pool classifiers can be made dense without changing their
canonical perspective prediction: the measured discrepancy is at floating
point round-off scale.  Second, every spatial operator in all three visual
backbones can be replaced by the same spherical sampling family while retaining
the learned parameters.  Third, a large equirectangular image can be processed
as a single tensor and yields a directional class-score lattice whose size is
governed by network stride rather than the canonical $224\!\times\!224$
training crop.

This directly answers why a high-resolution panorama still gives a seemingly
small map.  The classifier is not convolving with one $224\!\times\!224$ filter.
Instead, repeated strides reduce each dimension by approximately 32 before the
dense head.  A $224\!\times\!224$ canonical crop produces a $7\!\times\!7$
feature lattice before global pooling; doubling spatial input dimensions
approximately doubles lattice dimensions.  The $16\!\times\!32$ result for a
$512\!\times\!1024$ panorama is therefore the expected fully convolutional
geometry.

\subsection{Why the three models are complementary}

The ImageNet model can reveal directions associated with many concrete object
categories, but its single-label training and object vocabulary are an awkward
fit for broad indoor context.  Places365 is naturally suited to scene regions
and performs strongly on the selected lobby example, yet cannot ask for a
concept outside its 365 labels.  OpenCLIP accepts new text prompts without
retraining, but its scores depend on prompt wording and the comparison set.
Moreover, the local dense head discards global attention interactions.  It is
best described as frozen prompt-conditioned local similarity, not as an exact
dense version of the published CLIP image score.

\subsection{What is not established}

The pilot does not establish localisation accuracy, calibrated class
probabilities, rotation equivariance, superiority over planar multi-face
inference, or semantic quality beyond the two displayed scenes.  In particular,
a plausible overlay can be produced for a low-ranked class because per-map
min--max normalisation always expands its internal contrast.  Any subsequent
study should report unnormalised evidence and class rank alongside the image.

The direct operator is geometrically motivated, but the weights were learned
on perspective images whose statistics differ from equirectangular panoramas.
Weight reuse therefore does not imply semantic invariance.  Near the poles, a
tangent kernel crosses a rapidly compressed equirectangular region; numerical
sampling remains finite and seam-aware, but the pretrained feature hierarchy
has never observed such inputs during training.

\subsection{Next quantitative experiment}

The decisive extension is a paired-mask evaluation on licensed panoramas for
which image and official semantic modalities can be matched by identifier.
For each compatible fixed label, the protocol should measure saliency mass
inside the target mask, pointing-game success, average precision, and ROC AUC.
Thresholded IoU should be reported over a threshold sweep rather than at a
hand-selected cut.  Latitude bands and seam-crossing objects should be analysed
separately.  The same panorama should then be evaluated by direct spherical,
cubemap, and overlapping gnomonic pipelines under matched angular sampling,
including projection time, peak memory, median latency, and 95th percentile
latency.  Stanford 2D--3D--S \cite{armeni2016stanford} and Matterport3D
\cite{chang2017matterport} are suitable only when their licensed RGB and label
modalities are both available.  Missing ground truth must remain missing; it
must not be replaced by a generative oracle.

\section{Reproducibility and Data Boundary}

Every tabulated number is stored in a machine-readable pilot record together
with environment versions, checkpoint SHA-256 values, input SHA-256 values,
and implementation-file hashes.  The two input panoramas and derived overlays
are CC0-derived.  Model checkpoints are downloaded from their upstream sources
into a user-controlled cache only after explicit acceptance of their terms,
then checked for exact size and SHA-256 identity.  Checkpoint bytes, Stanford
data, and Matterport3D data are not redistributed with the manuscript.

The source manuscript builds against the official January 2024 ISPRS class and
bibliography style fetched at build time and verified by checksum.  For
double-blind review, author identity and repository location are withheld; a
camera-ready version can disclose the implementation and complete execution
commands.  This manuscript itself contains no external model judgement: all
conclusions follow from numerical comparisons, measured execution properties,
label ranks, and explicitly qualitative human inspection.

\section{Conclusions}

Frozen perspective classifiers can be repurposed as native spherical semantic
direction sensors without creating perspective faces and without training.
ImageNet ResNet18 and Places365 ResNet18 admit an exact fully convolutional
conversion before spatial operators are moved to the sphere.  OpenCLIP RN50
adds a useful open-prompt interface, but its local projection is necessarily a
different operator from the original global attention pool.  Across two
$512\!\times\!1024$ panoramas, all three networks produce finite
$16\!\times\!32$ score lattices in one spherical pass, with one-thread CPU
means below one second in the tested environment.

The result is a feasibility demonstration, not a semantic-accuracy result.
Selected maps show that object, scene, and prompt evidence can be associated
with directions on the sphere, while ranks expose cases where a visually
contrasted map corresponds to weak global support.  A publishable comparative
claim now depends on paired official masks and a matched multi-face baseline,
not on an automatic vision-language judge.
